AUSTRALIA'S URBAN WILDLIFE · 2016–2025

Wild Cities: Who Still Lives Among Us?

From birds above suburban streets to reptiles, mammals and thousands of insect species, Australia's cities support a surprising range of recorded wildlife.

This visualisation explores wildlife observations across Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Adelaide, Perth, Hobart, Darwin and Canberra–Queanbeyan.

KEY FINDING

Brisbane recorded the highest species richness:
4,678 documented species.

This was the largest recorded total among the eight selected urban areas during 2016–2025.

HOW TO READ THIS

Recorded species richness represents distinct species documented in Atlas of Living Australia occurrence data. It does not represent
complete biodiversity or animal population size.

Different cities lead different parts of the urban wildlife community

Darker cells indicate higher recorded species richness relative to other cities within the same animal group.

KEY FINDING

Sydney leads birds, mammals and reptiles, while Brisbane records the greatest insect richness.

Brisbane's 4,097 recorded insect species help explain why it ranks highest overall in recorded species richness.

HOW TO READ THIS

Colour is normalised separately within each animal group. Darker cells indicate a city with higher recorded richness relative to other cities in the same column, while exact values are available on hover.

About the data

Wildlife occurrence records were sourced from the Atlas of Living Australia and spatially restricted to eight ABS Urban Centres and Localities. Threat status was matched against current EPBC threatened fauna listings from DCCEEW.

Data sources: Atlas of Living Australia · Australian Bureau of Statistics · Department of Climate Change, Energy, the Environment and Water.
